## Why one workflow and thirty days

The business case for AI depends on the quality and cost of work the enterprise can actually use. Review, correction, approvals and operational failures all affect the return. Adoption that lasts requires a disciplined way to establish where AI adds value, under what conditions and who takes responsibility.

Slow AI provides that discipline. Teams define success, examine the underlying problem, authorize bounded AI use, verify results and retain evidence supporting consequential decisions. Slow does not mean uniform friction. It means care at the joints, where judgment and authorization sit, and free movement everywhere else. Controls deepen with the consequences of the work.

For an enterprise this produces three practical advantages.

A clearer investment decision. Agreed acceptance criteria and performance measures show whether AI improves usable output after review and rework.

A workable agreement across functions. Business, technology, legal, risk and security teams settle permitted uses, data boundaries, decision rights and escalation conditions before deployment.

A method the team keeps. Employees learn a repeatable discipline, and the workflows, evidence and review practices carry forward as tools change.

The initial engagement runs 30 days, scoped around one priority workflow and a named business owner. It establishes the baseline, trains the team on its actual work, implements proportionate controls and evaluates results. Deliverables: a documented workflow, human-authored acceptance criteria, role assignments, an evidence register, review and corrective-action procedures and a recommendation to expand, revise or stop. Success is assessed through cycle time, output quality, review effort, rework and control performance.

GRC Solutions Korea brings its founder's more than 25 years of legal, risk and cross-border operating experience, combined with AI audit practice and a maintained set of methods and tools. The Slow AI Kitchen supports learning and application; the [reference library](https://library.grcskorea.com/) connects that work to enterprise governance, and [The Handover](https://redcaps.substack.com/p/the-handover) is the user's guide to the whole account.

The governing commitments are explicit. Informed Intent requires prior authorization stating purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human in a position to give it. Final Liability rests with the Human attaches every material outcome to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene.

Start with one valuable workflow. Demonstrate the benefit, establish the controls and give management the evidence to decide what comes next. In the kitchen's own idiom this is a soft opening: one dish on a limited menu, served to invited guests, before the decision to open.

## Purpose

Use this workbook to establish whether a priority business workflow can benefit from AI within boundaries the enterprise can authorize, verify and sustain. It turns the proposal into a consultant delivery sequence, practical exercises, working records and a documented decision at day 30.

The engagement produces an initial operating model for the selected workflow, evidence of performance and a handover to named Client owners. Expansion depends on the results and a fresh authorization of the next scope.

## Consultant operating brief

Source basis: [Slow AI Kitchen](https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) · [GRC Workbook](https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) · [AI GRC Copilot](https://github.com/rolldabones/AI-GRC-Copilot)

**The assignment**

Help a named business owner determine whether a defined use of AI improves usable work after preparation, review, correction and oversight. Make the operating boundaries and evidence practical enough for the Client to maintain after the consultant leaves.

**Before the engagement starts**

- Confirm sponsor access, a business owner, a qualified reviewer, a technical owner and available operators. Agree time commitments and decision dates in W01 and W04.

- Identify the approved workspace, source documents, data permissions and evidence store. Use synthetic or approved redacted material until real-data use is authorized.

- Request existing workflow procedures, example outputs, incident records, applicable policies, tool/vendor records, training evidence and baseline timings. Record missing items in W14.

**Consultant responsibility**

Facilitate, challenge, document and test. The Client retains business decisions, risk acceptance and operational authorization. Legal applicability and legal effect are for the Client's counsel or qualified local counsel; items with legal effect carry the note: confirm with external counsel. A separate reviewer should challenge the recommendation; preserve Internal Audit independence if assurance is in scope.

**Scope and dependencies**

Thirty days is an initial delivery period for the agreed workflow. Integration, security remediation, specialist legal assessment and independent certification require their own scope where needed. Missing mandatory evidence blocks the dependent activity; adjust the scope or schedule through W20.

## Thirty day delivery cadence

Source basis: [Slow AI Kitchen](https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) · [GRC Workbook](https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook) · [GRC Solutions Korea practice](https://library.grcskorea.com/practice)

Days are elapsed engagement days. Place sessions on Client working days and confirm actual dates in W01. Meeting durations below are delivery planning defaults.

| **Period** | **Consultant and Client work** | **Decision and evidence** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1 to 5 | Kickoff 60 min; interviews and current-work walkthrough; compare candidate workflows; agree owners and baseline. | Scope agreed. W01 to W05 and W15 started. Mandatory unknowns assigned. |
| Days 6 to 10 | Tool/data review; human-authored acceptance criteria; full-day workshop using authorized material. | Bounded rehearsal authorized. W06 to W11, W12 test plan and W22 competence evidence. |
| Days 11 to 20 | Run matched baseline and supervised AI cases; weekly clinic; collect failures and corrections as well as successes. | Review at days 15 and 20. W13 to W19. Pause affected work for hard-stop triggers. |
| Days 21 to 25 | Analyze performance and uncertainty; test fallback, change response and ownership coverage; remediate and retest. | Evidence challenged. W17, W19 to W22 current; open limits explicit. |
| Days 26 to 30 | Independent challenge; sponsor decision; operating handover; schedule continuing review. | Expand specified scope, revise or stop. W23 and W24 signed; evidence pack transferred. |

**Checkpoints are engagement reviews**

They do not replace Kitchen gates. Organizational permission, trained operators and a review mechanism must exist before the first organizational use of AI, including a pilot. Each task still follows the applicable task gates.

## The task method and its evidence

Source basis: [Slow AI Kitchen](https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) · [Definition of Done](https://github.com/rolldabones/definition-of-done)

Canonical Steps 1 to 9 govern a work product. Steps 1 and 7 are production steps; the other seven are gates. W references point to this workbook.

| **Step** | **Gate and human work** | **Evidence** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Define the Dish | State the business task, audience, constraints and decision. | W03 task brief |
| 2 Mise en Place | Gate 1: define observable acceptance tests before AI. | W08 human-authored DoD |
| 3 Knife Work | Gate 2: personally read, reason, outline or calculate. | W09 first pass |
| 4 Read the Kitchen | Gate 3: examine context, affected people and failure. | W03 and W05 risk map |
| 5 Apply Heat | Gate 4: use AI within the authorized frame. | W10 authorization; W11 run record |
| 6 Name the Chef de Partie | Gate 5: confirm the named owner and authority for this outcome. | W04 and W13 |
| 7 Build the Plate | Produce the versioned work product against the agreed tests. | W11 output reference |
| 8 Taste Before Service | Gate 6: verify evidence, logic, arithmetic and fitness for use. | W12 and W13 validation |
| 9 Open the Pass | Gate 7: human confirmation before reliance; review what happened. | W13 release; W18 learning; Service Record (required Tier 3, recommended Tier 2) |

Prior authorization names the accountable human before the system acts. Step 6 confirms task-level ownership; it does not permit an unowned pilot to begin. Humans retain authorship of the Definition of Done and final confirmation. The manual first pass remains human work. The service closes with a [Service Record](./service-record.md), written by the named owner within one day of release; ten records trigger a method review under Step 12.

Use LEARN for context and stakeholders; ALIGN for objectives, tolerances and responses; PERFORM for controls, training and response; REVIEW for evidence and improvement. Feed learning back into the cycle.

**The institutional program**

Slow AI requires governed, explainable and auditable use. ## Facilitator instructions and weekly clinics

Source basis: [Slow AI Kitchen](https://github.com/rolldabones/slow-ai-kitchen) · [Definition of Done](https://github.com/rolldabones/definition-of-done) · [GRC Workbook](https://github.com/rolldabones/grc-workbook)

**Run the four exercises**

- Exercise 1: keep AI unused. Each participant frames the task, writes tests and prepares a first pass. Ask what consequence each test protects against. The named human settles the tests.

- Exercise 2: trace a plausible error into the next business decision. Identify affected people, the permitted data and tool, the accountable owner and the boundary that stops escalation of the error.

- Exercise 3: verify a material claim and a calculation against sources, test an edge case, retain corrections and practice refusing an unfit result. A justified refusal is a valid learning outcome.

- Exercise 4: introduce a model change or owner absence. Require the team to show who can pause use, operate the fallback and authorize resumption.

**Run a weekly 45 minute clinic**

Spend 10 minutes on results and missing evidence, 15 on a failed or disputed case, 10 on controls and changes and 10 on decisions, owners and deadlines. Complete W18 before closing. Use the meeting to inspect records and rehearse decisions.

**Questions that expose weak governance**

Which source supports this claim? Who can refuse this release? What did we discard before we understood it? What changes if the tool acts instead of drafts? What would make us retract permission? Who reads the alert and has time to respond?

## Worked case purpose and authorization

Fictional teaching case. All roles, records, numbers and thresholds in the worked case are illustrative. They are not Client results or evidence of framework effectiveness.

**Internal supplier dossier summaries**

Procurement analysts prepare a cited one-page factual summary for internal use. Procurement decisions remain with qualified humans using the original dossier. The rehearsal uses 20 synthetic cases in an approved environment. It excludes vendor ranking, approval, rejection, legal advice, sanctions determinations and external communications.

Illustrative classification: Kitchen task Tier 2 and GRC operating Tier 1. The classification depends on actual consequence and must be reassessed if the summary is used to determine supplier eligibility. Named fictional roles: procurement operations manager owns the workflow; procurement director authorizes scope; two trained analysts prepare drafts; a different analyst reviews each output; IT/security owns tool evidence.

**Acceptance criteria set by the fictional team**

- At least 18 of 20 first drafts meet all mandatory content tests without substantive correction. The manual comparator is 18 of 20.

- Every released output has traceable material claims, all mandatory fields, no material fabrication or omission and a recorded review and disposition.

- No unauthorized input, recipient, tool or external action. A critical boundary failure stops the affected activity.

- Mean total active human time falls by at least 20%, including preparation, review, correction and fallback.

- Before wider use, vendor/model-change evidence is verified and change response and manual fallback are demonstrated.

## Worked case comparable observations

Fictional measurements. Five cases per difficulty group. Different analysts perform manual and AI versions of each case; assignments and order are balanced. Scoring is blinded where feasible. All attempts and correction time are retained.

| **Case group** | **Cases** | **Manual min** | **AI min** | **Manual first pass** | **AI first pass** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Short complete | 5 | 150 | 100 | 5/5 | 4/5 |
| Routine | 5 | 200 | 125 | 5/5 | 5/5 |
| Long | 5 | 250 | 150 | 4/5 | 4/5 |
| Conflicting or incomplete | 5 | 300 | 225 | 4/5 | 3/5 |
| Total | 20 | 900 | 600 | 18/20 | 16/20 |

| **Human effort category** | **Manual minutes** | **AI minutes** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation and production | 600 | 230 |
| Initial independent review | 200 | 220 |
| Correction and re-review | 100 | 150 |
| Total | 900 | 600 |

AI preparation and production includes 100 minutes of human framing/source work plus 130 minutes of active interaction and draft handling. Final case approval is included in review. Categories are counted once.

Mean active effort is 45 minutes manually and 30 minutes with AI. Case-processing saving is 900 minus 600 = 300 minutes, or 5 hours. Reduction is 300 divided by 900 = 33.3%. Reviewer and correction effort increased and is already included.

One-time setup, training and control design consumed 16 person-hours. The 5-hour case saving minus 16 invested is an 11-hour deficit before additional recurring oversight. Oversight effort and tool fees are unmeasured, so full net benefit is incomplete. Capacity released is not a realized cash saving.

The sample shows neither annual savings nor rare-event safety. Elapsed turnaround and population variability would need their own evidence.
