## Why a Coda

The method ends at the Pass, or, for an institution, at the review cycle of Step 12. Every gate has been cleared and every record kept. What is left is the person who did it.

The more I listen to "Tom Sawyer" by Rush, the more I think it speaks to working with AI today. The song is a portrait of a modern individualist: reserved and hard to read, often mistaken for arrogant and owned by no one. My first reading took him for the machine, restless and everywhere. That reading is backward. The machine is the thing that can be rented. Tom Sawyer is the one thing in the song that cannot be. He is the Human the method needs us to be.

A coda is the right form for that. It comes after the piece has been played. It restates rather than develops, so that the listener leaves with the theme. The theme of this Kitchen is that responsibility stays human. The coda is a reminder to be the kind of person who can carry it.

This is not a call for heroes. The method is built so that ordinary people can carry that responsibility on an ordinary day: the gates do the remembering, and the records do the explaining. What the gates cannot supply is the disposition to use them. The coda is about that disposition.

## I. Two Tom Sawyers

There are two Tom Sawyers, and the method needs both of them.

The first is Mark Twain's, from 1876. In Chapter II he is sentenced to whitewash "Thirty yards of board fence nine feet high," and by the end of the afternoon the work is done, nearly every stroke of it by somebody else. He has made the job look like a privilege, and the boys of the village pay him in apples, marbles and a kite for a turn with the brush. Twain gives the lesson in his own voice: Tom "had discovered a great law of human action, without knowing it -- namely, that in order to make a man or a boy covet a thing, it is only necessary to make the thing difficult to attain." He adds that "Work consists of whatever a body is obliged to do, and that Play consists of whatever a body is not obliged to do." The fence ends with "three coats of whitewash on it."

Twain's Tom is a delegator, and the episode is a small governance case. Chapter III shows the inspection. Tom tells Aunt Polly it is all done. "Aunt Polly placed small trust in such evidence. She went out to see for herself." She found the fence "not only whitewashed but elaborately coated and recoated," and she credited Tom.

Read that as a reviewer would. Aunt Polly did the right thing: she did not take the claimant's word and checked against something outside it, which is what Gate 6 asks. The output passed. And her record was still wrong, because she inspected the fence and not the provenance of the work. Nobody told her who held the brush. Tom took the credit. Had one of the boys painted the fence badly, Tom would have answered for that too. **Final Liability** attached to Tom whoever did the painting. That much has not changed in a hundred and fifty years.

The second Tom Sawyer is the song's, from 1981. "Tom Sawyer" opens the Rush album *Moving Pictures*. The band, Geddy Lee, Alex Lifeson and Neil Peart, wrote it with the lyricist Pye Dubois, who brought Peart the poem the song grew from. Peart said Dubois's draft portrayed a modern rebel, free-spirited and individualist, and that he added two themes of his own: reconciling the boy with the man in himself, and the gap between what people are and what others take them to be. Peart died in January 2020. Dubois's death, at 77, was reported in September 2026.

Twain's boy got other people to do his work. The song's man will not let anyone do his thinking. An enterprise that uses AI is Twain's Tom at scale. It hands the fence to Tiny Servants that do not get tired. The coda asks it to be the song's Tom too.

## II. A Mind Not for Rent

The song's central claim about its hero is that his mind is "not for rent," whether to a god or to a government.

That is the exact opposite of an AI agent, and the method depends on the difference. *The Boundary and the Limit* states the one fact that AI changes: an agent takes direction and uses discretion without being employed. Discretion used to come only with a person attached. Now it can be rented by the hour, and the enterprise should rent it. The paper's rule is to rent the model and own the specification, the evidence, the permissions and the exit.

What cannot be rented is the judgment that answers for the result. The doctrine puts it in one sentence. Final Liability rests with the Human: every material outcome attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene. The owner is named at Step 1 and confirmed at Step 6, Name the Chef de Partie, Gate 5. Whoever stands there needs a mind that is their own. An owner who adopts the machine's conclusion because it is fluent, or the vendor's because it is convenient, or the organization's because it is safe, has rented out the one thing the gate exists to keep.

The fifth operating rule says the same thing in the method's own words: keep responsibility human. AI can assist, draft, simulate and challenge. It does not own the decision.

## III. The Company He Keeps

The refrain says that what you say about his company is what you say about society. It works for an enterprise at two scales.

At the scale of one piece of work, it is Gate 3, Read the Kitchen. An output is known by what it touches: who relies on it, what they will do with it and who is hurt if it is wrong. The company a piece of work keeps after it leaves the Pass is part of what it is.

At the scale of the firm, it is the boundary. *The Boundary and the Limit* separates the firm that answers from the firm that can govern, and calls the gap exposure. The agents a firm deploys are its company. What they do in its name is what the world will say about the firm, whether or not the firm could see them do it.

Jerry Evensky taught the same point through Walt Kelly's line for the first Earth Day in 1970: "We have met the enemy and he is us." The verdict on the machine comes back to the people who direct it.

## IV. What the Machine Runs On

The song's hero lives off the people and the energy around him. Hear those lines as the machine speaking and they describe the scarcest input in the method.

An agent runs on attention. It needs a person to frame the task, read the sources, judge the draft and decide whether to release it. *The Boundary and the Limit* makes that the limit of the governed firm: once execution can be rented, how much the firm can safely do is set by the time named, qualified people have to authorize work, confirm it and answer for it. Past that limit the cost of efficiency falls on people who cannot carry it.

That is what **Slow AI** protects. Slow AI: AI that cannot be governed, explained and audited is not deployed; the three tests are conjunctive, and the doctrine is a precondition, not a speed limit. The point is not to slow the machine. It is to keep the Human able to follow it. The first operating rule, think before prompting, is where that begins. Attention spent before the machine is engaged is the attention the machine cannot supply.

The song's Tom keeps his own counsel. The Human the method needs spends attention as if it were scarce, because it is.

## V. Reserve Is Not Arrogance

The song asks the listener not to mistake its hero's reserve for arrogance. His quiet is a defense, and it lets him ride out the day without being carried by it.

Anyone who has held a release knows the charge. The reviewer who will not sign until the evidence is in, the owner who asks for one more check, the lawyer who says "not yet" in a meeting that wants "yes": each is called difficult before the outcome shows they were right. Gate 6 asks for verification against something outside the model, and Gate 7 for a release decision made by a person. Both need someone willing to be thought slow.

Reserve is also what keeps the Human from being carried along by the session. Coetzee's Kitchen draws the line: an objective revised deliberately, because new information warrants it, is learning; an objective that drifts because the interaction carried it there may be loss of control. A quiet mind notices the drift.

## VI. Change Is the Only Fixture

The song's hero is hopeful and discontented at once, and he knows that no change lasts, though change itself does.

That is the right stance toward AI, and a hard one to keep. The tools improve each quarter, and so do the claims made for them. Products are withdrawn, companies absorbed and forecasts revised before the year is out. A practitioner who is only hopeful adopts everything. One who is only discontented adopts nothing. The method asks for both: use the tool, and expect it to change under you.

The Kitchen builds that expectation in. The [Your Restaurant](./README.md#addendum-your-restaurant) addendum treats every deployed system as having a lifecycle, from authorization through version tracking and monitoring to decommissioning. Under **GRCnext™**, a contingency plan that cannot be executed under stress is not optionality. It is documentation. Assume the change, and keep the choices that let you meet it.

## VII. The Exit

The song ends with its warrior leaving the stage. The method has an exit too, and it is where the song and the method agree most closely.

**Informed Intent** requires an exit before anything begins. Informed Intent: no AI system acts without prior authorization stating its purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, given by a human who meets the five authorizer conditions. The exit is the last of the four elements, and the one most often left blank. A system with no exit leaves its operator no way out, and Section VI says why there must be one.

The Human leaves too. People change roles, retire and move on. The method expects it. The allocation lifecycle of Final Liability runs: named at specification, attaches at confirmation, holds through reliance. Reliance can last long after the owner has gone. That is why the gates leave records. When the warrior exits, the record has to be able to say who answered and why.

## VIII. The Seat

Neil Peart, who wrote the song's words with Dubois, left the stage for good. He last played with Rush in 2015 and died in 2020, and the chair behind the kit stayed empty until the band went back on the road with Anika Nilles. The Fifty Something tour opened on 7 June 2026 at the Kia Forum in Los Angeles.

I wrote about that on LinkedIn, in ["Rush did not replace Neil Peart. They integrated Anika Nilles."](https://lnkd.in/p/gxgCztgx), and the post carries the coda's last lesson. Replacement asks the newcomer to become someone else. Integration asks the organization to make room. The band's rule: true to the arrangements first, because that is what the fans expect, and once she was comfortable and confident in them, free to enhance them with her own spirit. She was handed five songs, not fifty years, with people in the room to say this part is right and this part is this long. Geddy Lee's rule was that she would make mistakes and that what mattered was the recovery, so keep playing. And asked about new music, he said there was enough to do remembering the old ones.

That is how an enterprise should bring AI into a going concern. The arrangements are the method's: the steps, the gates and the Definition of Done. A new tool plays them faithfully before it is allowed to improvise. The scope is narrow, five songs at a time, starting where a wrong note costs least. A named owner and a named reviewer stand in the room to correct and reassure, which is Gate 5 and Gate 6. One song is played in public before the full show, which is Gate 7 for one piece of work and, for an institution, the registry and staffing of Steps 10 and 11 before the program runs at Step 12. New songs wait. That is Slow AI at the scale of a band: not slower, but with the people who answer for the music able to follow it.

The post's last turn is the one this coda needs. A named Human decided, and answers for it. Nobody replaced Neil, and nothing replaces the Human whose mind is not for rent. What the band did was make room for a new player while the people who answer for the music stayed in the room. An enterprise should ask no more of AI than a seat in the band, and no less of itself than to keep the people who answer in the room.

## The Ode

This is the part to keep.

When you work with AI, keep your mind your own. Rent the machine's discretion and never your judgment.

Know the company your work keeps. What it touches is part of what it is, and what it does in your name is said about you.

Spend your attention as if it were scarce, because it is the one thing the machine runs on that it cannot make.

Hold your reserve. Say "not yet" when the evidence is thin, and let them call it arrogance until the record shows otherwise.

Be hopeful and discontented at once. Use the tool, and expect it to change.

Build the exit before you need it, and leave a record good enough to answer for you after you have gone.

When something new takes a seat, integrate it. Do not ask it to become someone else, and do not let it become you.

Twain's Tom got the fence painted by others and took the credit. Be the other Tom Sawyer. Let the machine hold the brush, and answer for the fence yourself.

## The Sign

In written music the coda sign marks the place where the player leaves the main body of the piece and jumps to the closing passage. The Kitchen uses it the same way. The sign appears at the end of the twelve steps in the [README](./README.md), with the words "To Coda", and at the head of this file. A reader who has finished the method follows the sign here.

The sign is drawn as an image, [coda-sign.svg](./coda-sign.svg), a circle crossed by a vertical and a horizontal line. Unicode has a character for it, U+1D10C MUSICAL SYMBOL CODA, but the fonts GitHub asks for do not carry it. On 17 November 2026 (KST) it rendered as an empty placeholder box in a desktop browser on macOS, so the repository uses the image, with "Coda sign" as its alternative text and the word "Coda" beside it wherever it appears.

The sign marks only this passage. It is not a gate, a step or a mark of status, and no other file uses it.

## A Note on Provenance

In the spirit of Coetzee's Kitchen, the lineage of the coda is stated.

The starting point was mine: the conviction that the song has a lot to say about AI today. So were the ruling that its hero "is the Human that we need to be," the LinkedIn post on Anika Nilles and its comments, from which Section VIII takes its argument and most of its words, the request for an entry that reminds readers to strive to be what the song describes when they work with AI, and the decisions to make it a coda and to use the coda sign in the Kitchen. So are *The Boundary and the Limit* with its Tiny Servants and its two boundaries, the three doctrines and the Evensky lesson with Walt Kelly's line, which the coda takes from that paper.

The machine supplied the reading on which the coda turns: that the song's hero is not the AI but its inverse, the one thing in the song that cannot be rented. It proposed the passages read against each gate, and the use of Twain's fence and Aunt Polly's inspection as a governance case. It drafted the prose and the closing ode, which I reviewed. It also caught a conflict in its sources: Dubois's original poem is reported as "Louis the Lawyer" in some accounts and "Louie the Warrior" in others, and some accounts that use each call the other a misattribution. The coda does not name the poem. The machine also tested the Unicode coda sign, found that it did not render and drew the image that replaces it. Accepting the machine's contributions was my decision, and so is the coda.
