## Preface: The Object of Governance

Artificial intelligence governance is usually framed as the governance of a technology: the model, its capabilities, its outputs and its risks. Accuracy, bias, hallucination, robustness and capability all matter. None of them is where consequence arises.

A model considered in isolation does very little. It becomes consequential when it is embedded in a system that contains data, instructions, tools, permissions, workflows, people and access to an external environment. The object of governance is not intelligence in the abstract. It is consequential machine action.

The unit of analysis is therefore not the model. It is the agent in motion, and the motion has a shape:

**Intent → Authority → Action → Consequence**

The chain is ordinal. It says what precedes what. It assigns no quantities, and none should be attached to it.

Newton's achievement was not a list of effects. It was a system: bodies, forces and motions, related through three laws of motion and one law of force, general enough to carry the same reasoning from a falling body to the orbit of the Moon. That is the method borrowed here. The governance problem is to keep the movement from intent to consequence bounded, observable, interruptible and attributable along its whole trajectory, and to say so in as few rules as will hold.

This reframes the central question. Instead of asking only how capable the model is, we ask what the agent is permitted to do, within what boundaries, producing what consequences and under whose authority. The definitions fix those terms. The laws relate them.

## Definitions

**I. Agent.** An AI system embedded with data, instructions, tools, permissions and workflows and able to act on an environment, whether by executing an action or by producing output that a person acts on. The model alone is not the agent.

**II. Authority.** The actions, systems, data and channels of influence an agent can reach, whether by permission or in effect. Authority includes influence. A system that only advises holds authority over whatever its users do on its advice without independent judgment.

**III. Reach.** The part of the world an agent's authority can change. Reach is counted across every agent acting on the same environment rather than one agent at a time, because many narrowly authorized systems can produce together a consequence none could produce alone.

**IV. Motion.** The agent's passage from intent through authority and action to consequence. Motion is what governance governs.

**V. Consequence.** A change in the world produced by the motion. A consequence is material at the threshold the specification sets for the outcomes that attach to a named owner. This module does not set the threshold. Corollary V states what constrains it.

**VI. Owner.** The named human to whom material outcomes attach, holding decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene. Authority reaches the agent only through named humans: the authorizer who confers it in the specification and the owner who holds it while the agent moves, who may be and often are one person. It has no other source. The mechanics also calls the owner the principal, borrowing the vocabulary of the law of agency, in which authority flows from principal to agent and the principal answers for acts within it. The borrowing is of vocabulary only. Owner and principal name the same person, and the doctrine's term controls.

**VII. Specification.** The written authorization by which Informed Intent is given: purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, with the criteria against which outcomes will be confirmed. Its drafting is the subject of [computational drafting](https://github.com/rolldabones/computational-drafting).

**VIII. Slow AI.** The name of the practice as a whole and, within it, of the doctrine stated at DOCTRINE.md §1. In this module the practice is the mechanics and the doctrine is the Second Law.

## Law I: Intent

***An agent's default state is rest. It moves only when authorized and only as authorized, and it stops at the exit.***

**Doctrine.** No consequential AI without **Informed Intent**: prior authorization that specifies what the system may do, on whose authority, within what boundaries and with what exit. Four elements, all required ([DOCTRINE.md](https://github.com/rolldabones/rolldabones/blob/main/DOCTRINE.md) §2). Define the trajectory before permitting the motion.

Newton's mechanics begins from a state a body keeps unless something acts on it. Governance begins from the same place. Nothing acts on the organization's behalf until authorized, and the authorization is the reference against which every later observation is read. Without it an organization can record what the system did but cannot say whether the system did what was intended, and governance collapses into retrospective explanation.

At the altitude of a working deployment, the four elements unfold into the specification the authorizer writes before the agent moves.

| Element | What the specification states |
|---|---|
| Purpose | The objective and the criteria by which performance will be judged |
| Authority | On whose authority the agent moves and who is named to own its outcomes |
| Boundaries | The permitted actions and the prohibited actions; the systems and data in reach; the evidence required before a material decision |
| Exit | The conditions that require human intervention and the conditions that require termination |

An authorization without an exit is not an authorization. It is a hope. A general permission to use AI authorizes nothing in particular and therefore authorizes nothing.

The agentic case is the hard case and the reason the law is stated at this strength. A system that plans across steps and calls tools will reach states its authorizer never contemplated. It will stray from the authorized motion, moved by what it meets on the way: an input, a tool result, another agent. The boundary and the exit are what make that survivable: the boundary limits how far, and the exit returns the agent to rest.

## Law II: Motion

***The consequence an agent can produce is bounded by its authority and its reach, not by its intelligence.***

**Doctrine.** No consequential motion by a system that cannot be governed, explained and audited, however fast it would make the business. That is **Slow AI** ([DOCTRINE.md](https://github.com/rolldabones/rolldabones/blob/main/DOCTRINE.md) §1). Govern the motion, not only the machine.

A highly capable model confined to a sandbox may have little ability to affect the world. A far less capable system permitted to execute payments, modify production systems, publish externally, disclose information or decide cases presents the greater operational risk. The critical variable is not what the system knows or what the model can generate. It is what the system can cause to happen. Hence the working heuristic: classify the agent by its verb. Pay, publish, disclose, decide. Govern the verb, the boundary and the consequence.

That gives a hierarchy of transitions.

Capability creates possibility. Authority creates agency. Action creates exposure. Consequence creates accountability.

Governance operates at the transitions. Permissions, isolation, least privilege, approval gates, monitoring, logging, escalation and termination rights are sized to the consequence the motion can produce. The greater the authority and the reach, the stronger the required control. Because consequence is bounded by authority and reach, that is where governance must bind: on what the agent can do and where, not on what it knows. The three tests of the doctrine are tests of the motion.

Two consequences follow from the definitions. Because authority includes influence, a system that only advises is governed at the point where a person acts on it: not by permissions, which do not reach that point, but by trained users and by the evidence the specification requires before a material decision. Automation bias is a Second Law problem, and it lives on the human side of the transition. And because reach is measured across the fleet, an organization that governs each agent well and never adds them up has not governed its reach.

Slow AI is not a speed limit. It is a precondition, and its three tests are conjunctive: a system that is explainable but unauditable fails, and so does one that is auditable but ungoverned. The point is not to make the machine slower. It is to keep the Human able to follow it, to intervene where intervention matters and to answer for the result.

## Law III: Liability

***Delegation transfers action. It does not transfer accountability.***

**Doctrine.** No material consequence without **Final Liability**: every material outcome produced by an AI system attaches to a named human owner with decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene ([DOCTRINE.md](https://github.com/rolldabones/rolldabones/blob/main/DOCTRINE.md) §3). There is no such thing as an outcome the model is responsible for.

An AI system can exercise delegated agency. It cannot absorb institutional responsibility, and it cannot be made to. Systems are not liability-bearing entities. For every material domain in which an agent may act there is therefore a named human owner, and the naming happens at specification, as part of the authorization. Naming after the fact is selection of a scapegoat, not allocation of accountability.

The name is necessary and not sufficient. The owner holds three attributes, all of them: decision rights over the delegation; oversight of the system's material behavior, with access to the evidence needed to exercise judgment; and the power to constrain or terminate the system. A person who is named but holds none of these is a nominee, and nominating one is worse than naming nobody, because it manufactures the appearance of accountability.

Final Liability does not require a human to confirm every machine action. That would remove most of the value of delegation. It requires something more precise. Every material outcome is confirmed by the named owner against criteria defined in advance, with three lawful outcomes (confirm, refuse, or amend and confirm), each with a record and no fourth. And accountability, once attached, holds for as long as the organization or anyone else relies on the output, which is usually far longer than the project that produced it.

The motion ends. The reliance does not, and the accountability runs with the reliance.

## Corollaries

**Corollary I. The architecture.** The three laws produce a simple structure:

**Informed Intent → Governed Authority → Observable Action → Final Liability**

Informed Intent establishes where the system is supposed to go. Authority determines how much of the world it can affect. Slow AI governs its movement through that world. Evidence and logs make the trajectory reconstructable. Human gates permit intervention where consequence warrants it. Final Liability names the person who owns the delegation and its material outcomes, from specification, through confirmation, for as long as anyone relies on the result.

**Corollary II. The test.** The purpose of governance is not to eliminate machine motion. It is to make the motion governable. A governed agent is:

**Bounded. Observable. Interruptible. Attributable.**

Bounded: the specification's boundaries hold while the agent moves. Observable: the motion leaves a record as it moves, before anyone asks for one. Interruptible: a working intervention path can stop it. Attributable: a named owner holds it.

The four properties are properties of the motion, and they reduce to the doctrine rather than adding to it. Governed, in DOCTRINE.md, means a named owner, defined boundaries and a working intervention path: attributable, bounded and interruptible. Audited means the evidence exists before it is asked for: observable. Explained, the third test, is not a fifth property of the motion. It is a property of the account the owner can give of the motion: the record, read against the specification, stated in terms the affected person can use. Observation supplies the record, Informed Intent supplies the reference and the owner supplies the statement. All three tests remain conjunctive. The four properties are what governed and audited look like while the agent moves.

The test is deliberately not prediction. Models are probabilistic and agents reach states nobody specified. What the test asks is whether the motion, whatever it turns out to be, can be bounded, seen, stopped and owned.

**Corollary III. Friction.** Every control the Second Law sizes to consequence is friction: a confirmation, a boundary, a delay, a stop. In engineering, friction is usually something to be reduced, and digital transformation inherited the instinct. Faster is presumed better. Fewer approvals are presumed better. Automation is justified precisely because it removes human friction.

For consequential AI the assumption is incomplete. Some friction is waste. Some friction is control.

A human confirmation before an irreversible payment, a permission boundary that keeps an agent away from sensitive data, a delay before generated content is published externally, an automatic stop when behavior departs from specified parameters: each reduces speed, and the reduction is the mechanism through which governance becomes possible. Newton gave his second book to the motion of bodies through resisting media. Governance is a resisting medium by design.

Slow AI therefore distinguishes unproductive friction from governance friction. The objective is not maximum friction and not maximum speed. It is the minimum friction necessary to preserve meaningful human control over material consequences.

**Corollary IV. The sequence.** The doctrines are sequential, not parallel, and a failure in any one collapses the others. An unauthorized system has no named owner, because there was no specification to name one in. An unauditable system cannot support confirmation, because there is nothing to confirm against. Where no human holds decision rights, the authorization was to nobody.

**Corollary V. Materiality.** The laws speak of consequential motion and material outcomes, and the specification draws the line between what is material and what is not (Definition V). That is the seam a hostile reader will press: an organization that wants no owner for an outcome need only draw the line above it. The mechanics does not draw the line. It says four things about it.

First, the line is drawn in the specification, before the motion, or it is not a line. The First Law names the owner at specification, and no owner can be named for outcomes the specification has not yet called material. A line drawn after the consequence is the same move as naming after the fact: the selection of an excuse rather than the allocation of accountability.

Second, the line is drawn on someone's authority, which the First Law requires the specification to state, and drawing it is itself a decision with a named human behind it. Whatever falls below the line was placed there by a person, on the record, and the placement is theirs. Lowering the accountability inside the system raises it on the one who drew the line.

Third, the line is measured on the world, not on the organization (Definition V), and across the fleet, not one agent at a time (Definition III). A line that counts only what the organization stands to lose measures the wrong thing, because the world includes the person affected. And a thousand consequences each below the line, produced on the same environment, are counted together. Slicing is not lowering.

Fourth, the record does not respect the line. Audited means the evidence exists before it is asked for, of the whole motion and not only of the part the specification called material (Corollary II). An organization that draws the line high keeps the evidence of what it excluded, and that evidence is what an auditor, a regulator or a court will read. Where a law has already defined which decisions count, its definition is a floor under the line, not a ceiling over it.

The line, then, may be drawn where the organization likes, and the mechanics will still say who drew it, what fell below it, on what evidence and to whom the choice belongs. That is not an escape. It is the Third Law applied to the line itself: drawing it is an action, and the accountability for it does not move.

## Scholium: What the Correspondence Is and Is Not

From Newton this module borrows the form (three laws) and the method (compression). It does not borrow the physics.

The First Law here has the shape of the law of inertia: a default state, and departure from it only under something impressed from outside. The Second has the shape of Newton's second: what can be produced answers to what is impressed, with authority standing where force stands. The Third has the shape of Newton's third: reciprocity, every delegation of action matched by a retention of accountability. The correspondence is in the shape of the relations and nowhere else. Authority is not a force, consequence is not a change of motion and responsibility is not a conserved quantity. This module makes no conservation claim, and a reader who finds one has read it in.

Newton's three laws say how bodies answer to force. Where force comes from took a separate law. This module needs none, because the source of authority is already in the definitions: it reaches the agent only through a named human, and it has no other source. An agent that acquires authority any other way, by misconfiguration, from another agent or from a user who never held it, is moving under a force the mechanics does not recognize, and the First Law says what follows. It is not authorized, and it returns to rest.

Nor is there a fourth law. Newton followed his definitions with a scholium on absolute and relative motion, and perspective matters in governance as frames of reference matter in mechanics. But turning every useful Newtonian concept into another law defeats the compression the method exists for.

## General Scholium: The Proposition

The analogy matters less for its vocabulary than for its method, and the method is compression. Rather than building ever larger catalogs of AI principles, controls and risks, the aim is a small number of relations from which appropriate controls can be derived.

Three laws.

**Law I. Intent.** An agent's default state is rest. No consequential AI without Informed Intent: purpose, authority, boundaries and exit, specified before the system moves.

**Law II. Motion.** What an agent can cause is bounded by its authority and its reach, not by its intelligence. No consequential motion by a system that cannot be governed, explained and audited.

**Law III. Liability.** Delegation transfers action, not accountability. No material consequence without a named human owner holding decision rights, oversight and the power to intervene, from specification, through confirmation, for as long as anyone relies on the result.

Together: Informed Intent sets the trajectory. Slow AI governs the motion. Final Liability owns the consequence.

The module does not ask an organization to govern artificial intelligence as an abstraction. Before the motion, it asks:

What is moving? What is causing it to move? What authority does it hold? What constrains its trajectory? What happens if it leaves that trajectory? Who can stop it? Where is the line of materiality, and who drew it? Who owns the consequence?

After a failure, the doctrine supplies the sequence, and it is the same chain read backwards: who was named, on what specification, what did they confirm, against what criteria and who has been relying on it since.

Newton closed the *Principia* by declining to frame a hypothesis about the cause of gravity, having described its effects exactly. This module closes with a refusal of its own. It frames no equation. The chain is ordinal, the laws are relations and the controls are derived from them, not computed. What remains is one proposition:

**Do not govern intelligence in the abstract. Govern consequential motion through the world.**

That is the Newtonian premise of Slow AI.
